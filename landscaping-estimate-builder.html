Practical quoting guide

What should a landscaping estimate include?

A useful landscaping quote spells out the job area, labor, materials, cleanup, and any assumptions before work begins.

1. Scope of work

Describe the property area and tasks, such as mowing, edging, weeding, planting, mulch, hauling, or seasonal cleanup.

2. Clear line-item prices

Break out labor, materials, delivery, and debris removal where relevant. Include applicable discounts and taxes.

3. Terms and validity

Record customer details, the quote date, validity period, timing assumptions, and any deposit expectations.

Before you price a landscaping job

Landscaping quotes often change when the scope or site conditions are unclear. Check these details before sending your estimate.

  1. Measure the area. Record lawn square footage, bed dimensions, or the number of plants and trees involved.
  2. Estimate materials. Calculate mulch volume, soil, plants, or other supplies, and note whether delivery is included.
  3. Plan labor and access. Consider crew size, equipment, slopes, gates, and parking or travel time.
  4. Define cleanup. Specify whether bagging, hauling, and disposal are included or separately charged.
  5. Set boundaries. Clarify weather-related rescheduling, changes in scope, and how long the estimate remains valid.
Helpful distinction: A one-time installation estimate may need material quantities and site preparation, while a recurring mowing quote should specify service frequency and what's included each visit.
